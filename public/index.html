Pest control near you, across the United States

Pest pressure is local. Formosan termites swarm along the Gulf Coast in spring, bark scorpions hide in Arizona block walls, brown recluse spiders fill storage rooms in Missouri and Oklahoma, and Northeast homes fight mice and blacklegged ticks. A provider who works in your area knows the local species, the season and the building styles that let pests in, which is why local expertise matters more than a national logo.

Whether you're in a high-rise apartment, a farmhouse, a ranch-style home on a slab or a restaurant kitchen, the goal is the same: find where pests are getting in and what's feeding them, treat them where they live, and keep them out.

What professional pest control looks like

  1. Inspection. A licensed technician checks inside and out for activity, entry points and conditions such as leaks, clutter or mulch against siding.
  2. Identification. The correct species decides the method. Pharaoh ants, German cockroaches and drywood termites each need a different approach.
  3. Targeted treatment. Following integrated pest management, products go where pests live: baits, crack-and-crevice treatments, soil barriers or traps.
  4. Exclusion and prevention. Sealing gaps, fixing moisture and adjusting storage keep new pests out.
  5. Follow-up. Monitoring and return visits confirm the problem is solved.

What affects the cost of pest control

  • The pest: termites, bed bugs and wildlife usually cost more than ants or spiders
  • How established the infestation is and how many rooms or units are affected
  • Home size, foundation type, and access to crawl spaces and attics
  • Treatment method, such as bait stations versus liquid barriers or heat versus chemical
  • One-time service versus a recurring plan with re-service included
  • Exclusion repairs, cleanup and warranties

Ask for a written estimate that lists the pests covered, products and methods, the number of visits and any guarantee before you agree to service. The EPA advises that cost should not be the only factor when choosing a pest control service.

Warning signs you need an exterminator

  • Droppings in cabinets, drawers or along baseboards
  • Mud tubes on the foundation or discarded insect wings on windowsills
  • Scratching or scurrying in walls or the attic at night
  • Rusty spots on mattress seams or unexplained bites after sleeping
  • Ant trails that return every day
  • Cockroaches seen in daylight
  • Gnawed wires, food packaging or wood
  • Wasps flying in and out of a gap in siding or the ground

Spotting any of these more than once is a good reason to call. Early treatment is usually simpler and less expensive than dealing with an established colony, nest or infestation.

Choosing a pest control company

Whoever you hire, a few checks protect you. Confirm the company holds the license your state requires. Ask which products they'll use and request the labels. Make sure the estimate is in writing, and understand what the guarantee covers. The National Pesticide Information Center recommends asking for references and checking your state pesticide regulatory office for complaints. Every provider in the PestHalt network is expected to meet these basics.

Pest control questions

How do I find pest control near me?

Call (800) 555-0199. PestHalt connects you with a state-licensed pest control provider who serves your city or county, so you skip the search and the round of callbacks.

How much does pest control cost?

Prices depend on the pest, the size of the home, how established the problem is, the treatment method and whether you choose a one-time visit or a recurring plan. Get a written estimate after an inspection before any work begins.

What pests do you help with?

General household insects such as ants, roaches and spiders, plus termites, rodents, bed bugs, mosquitoes, wasps and bees, fleas and ticks, wildlife and commercial pest programs.

Are pest control treatments safe for kids and pets?

Registered products applied according to the label are designed to be low risk once dry. Providers following integrated pest management favor baits, traps and targeted crack-and-crevice treatments indoors. Tell the technician about children, pets and anyone with health concerns.

How quickly can someone come out?

Scheduling depends on the provider in your area and the time of year. When you call, explain how urgent the problem is, such as stinging insects near a door or rodents in a kitchen, so it can be prioritized.

Is the pest control provider licensed?

Pest control is a licensed trade in every state. Providers in our network hold the license their state requires, and you can verify any license number with your state pest control regulator.

Do I need a recurring plan or a one-time treatment?

One-time visits suit a single problem like a wasp nest. Ants, roaches and spiders tend to return from outside, so quarterly plans are common. Termites and bed bugs follow their own treatment programs.

What should I do before the technician arrives?

Note where and when you see pests, keep pets secured, clear access to sinks, the garage, attic and crawl space, and do not spray store products right before an inspection, which can hide activity.
