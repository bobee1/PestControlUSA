Why pest control in Florida is different

Florida's subtropical climate, high humidity and frequent rain mean pests rarely take a season off. South Florida faces both Formosan and Asian subterranean termites, two invasive species that build huge colonies and can damage high-rise condos as well as single-family homes. Drywood termites are common in coastal areas, which is why tent fumigation is a familiar sight in Miami, Tampa and Fort Myers.

American and smoky brown roaches, which Floridians call palmetto bugs, move indoors after heavy rains. Red imported fire ants are everywhere from lawns to retention pond banks, and mosquito control districts operate across most counties to manage the state's year-round mosquito season. Slab-on-grade block homes, pool cages and lanais all create their own pest entry points.

Pest control tips for Florida residents

  • Trim palm fronds and tree limbs away from the roof to cut off roof rat highways.
  • Keep mulch and irrigation away from the slab edge; constant moisture draws subterranean termites.
  • Before a home sale, budget time for a wood-destroying organism (WDO) inspection on the state's official form.

Pest control licensing in Florida

Pest control in Florida is regulated by the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services, which licenses businesses and certified operators in categories including general household pest control, termite and wood-destroying organism control, lawn and ornamental, and fumigation. WDO inspections for real estate use the state's official inspection report form, and fumigation requires specific notices and safety procedures. Verify any company's FDACS license before you sign a contract.

Pest control in Florida: questions

Is pest control in Florida needed year-round?

For most homes, yes. Mild winters let roaches, ants and termites stay active, so quarterly or bi-monthly service is common, with termite protection handled through a separate warranty.

Do you cover all of Florida?

Yes. PestHalt connects callers in all 171 Florida cities and communities over 25,000 people, including Jacksonville, Miami, Tampa, Orlando, Saint Petersburg, Hialeah, and in smaller towns and rural areas served by local providers.

Who licenses pest control companies in Florida?

Pest control in Florida is licensed by the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS). Ask any provider for its license number and verify it with the agency before work begins.
