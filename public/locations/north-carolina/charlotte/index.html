Why pest control in Charlotte is different

Charlotte sits in the North Carolina Piedmont, with red clay soils, humid summers and many homes built on crawl spaces. Early-1900s bungalows in Dilworth, Elizabeth, Plaza Midwood and Wesley Heights have decades of termite exposure, while Myers Park and Eastover are shaded by willow oaks that drop leaves into gutters and hold moisture against roofs and foundations.

In 1989, Hurricane Hugo reached Charlotte with damaging winds that toppled thousands of trees, and the city has rebuilt its canopy since. Little Sugar Creek, Briar Creek and the greenway network bring mosquitoes, ticks and wildlife through neighborhoods. Charlotte has also been one of the fastest-growing cities in the country, and new subdivisions in Ballantyne, Steele Creek and University City disturb soil, pushing mice, fire ants and snakes toward new homes.

Growth, new construction and pests in Charlotte

Charlotte's growth means thousands of new homes each year on land that was recently woods or farmland. Clearing and grading displace mice, fire ants and other pests, and new homes often have small gaps around utility lines, garage doors and HVAC penetrations. Most new homes receive a termite pre-treatment, but warranties vary. In the first year, ask about the pre-treatment paperwork, seal gaps and start a recurring pest plan while landscaping matures.

Pest control tips for Charlotte residents

  • Have crawl spaces checked once a year for termite tubes and moisture.
  • Clean gutters under willow oaks every fall and spring.
  • Do tick checks after walking greenways and wooded trails.
  • In new homes, seal utility gaps and garage door edges before the first fall cold snap.

Pest control in Charlotte: common questions

Are termites common in Charlotte?

Yes. Subterranean termites are among the most common pest problems in the Charlotte area, especially in crawl-space homes.

Do Charlotte homes have fire ants?

Fire ants are established across the Charlotte region and build mounds in lawns, parks and along roads.

Why do I have mice in my new Charlotte house?

Land clearing pushes mice toward new homes, which often have small construction gaps. Exclusion and trapping usually fix it.
