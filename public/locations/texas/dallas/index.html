Why pest control in Dallas is different

Much of Dallas is built on the Blackland Prairie, whose heavy clay soils swell when wet and shrink in drought. That movement cracks slabs, separates expansion joints and opens hidden gaps that subterranean termites use to reach framing. The Trinity River bottoms, White Rock Lake and the many creeks threading through Lake Highlands and Oak Cliff add moisture and mosquito habitat.

The city's housing ranges from 1920s Tudors and bungalows in the M Streets, Lakewood and Kessler Park to postwar ranch homes and dense apartment blocks in Uptown and Far North Dallas. Dallas County was the center of a major West Nile virus outbreak in 2012, which led to aerial spraying, and the county still monitors mosquitoes each summer. Striped bark scorpions are found in older homes with rock landscaping and brick veneer, and fire ants are everywhere from front yards to soccer fields.

Black clay, foundations and termites in Dallas

Foundation repair and termite trouble often go together in Dallas. When clay soil shrinks in summer, gaps open around plumbing penetrations and along the slab edge; when fall rains return, termites forage right through those gaps. If you have had foundation piers installed or a slab leak repaired, ask whether a termite treatment was disturbed, since drilling and trenching can break the treated soil zone. Annual inspections are especially valuable on homes with past foundation work.

Pest control tips for Dallas residents

  • Run soaker hoses around the foundation in drought to limit slab movement and cracks.
  • Keep mulch thin and away from brick ledges; termites travel unseen behind it.
  • Use a fire ant bait across the whole yard in spring and fall rather than treating single mounds.
  • Empty birdbaths, gutters and plant trays weekly during mosquito season.

Pest control in Dallas: common questions

Why are termites so common in Dallas?

Native subterranean termites are active across North Texas, and the area's expansive clay soils crack slabs and open hidden entry points.

Are there scorpions in Dallas?

Yes. Striped bark scorpions live across Dallas and are most common in homes with rock landscaping, woodpiles or older brick veneer.

When is mosquito season in Dallas?

Usually from spring through the first cool weather in fall, peaking in mid-to-late summer when West Nile virus activity is highest.
