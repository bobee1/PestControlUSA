Why pest control in Pennsylvania is different

Pennsylvania was ground zero for two major invasive pests. Brown marmorated stink bugs were first found in the United States near Allentown in the mid-to-late 1990s, and spotted lanternflies were first found in Berks County in 2014. Both are now widespread, gathering on homes, trees and decks.

Pennsylvania has reported more Lyme disease cases than almost any other state, and blacklegged ticks are found in every county. Eastern subterranean termites are active statewide, and Philadelphia's row houses see rats, mice, roaches and bed bugs moving between connected homes.

Lanternflies, stink bugs and Lyme in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania has been at the front line of two invasive insects. The brown marmorated stink bug was first collected in the U.S. near Allentown in the late 1990s and now gathers on homes statewide every fall. The spotted lanternfly was found in Berks County in 2014, and the Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture now runs a quarantine across many counties, asking residents and businesses to check vehicles and goods before moving them out of infested areas.

Pennsylvania also reports some of the highest numbers of Lyme disease cases in the country, with blacklegged ticks common from the Poconos to the suburbs of Philadelphia and Pittsburgh. Its older housing stock, from Philadelphia rowhomes to Pittsburgh hillside homes and small-town brick houses, gives mice, carpenter ants and subterranean termites plenty to work with.

Pest control tips for Pennsylvania residents

  • Scrape lanternfly egg masses into alcohol in winter and check vehicles before leaving quarantine zones.
  • Seal around windows, siding and attic vents before stink bugs arrive.
  • Coordinate rodent work with neighbors in attached row houses.

Pest control licensing in Pennsylvania

The Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture licenses commercial pesticide businesses and certifies applicators, and runs a pesticide hypersensitivity registry for notification before nearby applications. Ask for the provider's PDA license.
