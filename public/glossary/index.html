Pest Control Glossary

55 pest control terms in plain English, from action threshold to yellow jacket.

Call (800) 555-0199

Pest control reports, contracts and product labels use a lot of technical words. This glossary explains them simply and links each term to the service page where it matters most. For anything else, browse all pest control services.

Jump to: A B C D E F G H I L M N O P R S T W Y

A

Action threshold
The level of pest activity at which treatment makes sense. In integrated pest management, one spider in a basement may be below the threshold, while one cockroach in a kitchen is above it. Related: General Pest Control
Adulticide
A pesticide that kills adult insects, such as mosquito barrier treatments, as opposed to larvicides that target immature stages. Related: Mosquito Control
Africanized honey bee
A hybrid honey bee, established in parts of the southern U.S., that defends its colony more aggressively than European honey bees. Related: Wasp & Bee Removal

B

Bait station
A tamper-resistant container that holds rodent or insect bait so that target pests can feed while children and pets cannot reach it. Related: Rodent Control
Barrier treatment
A residual product applied around a structure's foundation, or to yard foliage, to stop pests before they get inside or to kill resting mosquitoes. Related: Mosquito Control
Bed bug encasement
A zippered, bite-proof cover for mattresses and box springs that traps bed bugs inside and makes inspections easier. Related: Bed Bug Treatment
Blacklegged tick
Also called the deer tick, the species that spreads Lyme disease in the Northeast, Mid-Atlantic and upper Midwest. Related: Flea & Tick Control
Borate
A wood treatment made from boron salts that kills or repels wood-destroying insects and fungi; often used on exposed framing. Related: Termite Control
Brown recluse
A venomous spider with a violin-shaped mark, common in the south-central U.S., that hides in undisturbed clutter. Related: Spider Control
Bti
Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis, a naturally occurring bacterium used as a larvicide that kills mosquito larvae in standing water. Related: Mosquito Control

C

Carpenter ant
A large ant that hollows out damp or decaying wood to nest, leaving sawdust-like frass, without eating the wood. Related: Ant Control
Carpenter bee
A large bee that bores round, half-inch tunnels into exposed softwood such as decks, fascia and eaves. Related: Wasp & Bee Removal
Clean-out service
The first, more intensive visit in a recurring pest plan, designed to knock down an existing infestation before routine service. Related: General Pest Control
Cluster flies
Large flies that spend the winter in attics and wall voids and appear on sunny windows on warm days. Related: General Pest Control
Conducive conditions
Situations that attract or support pests, such as leaks, wood touching soil, clutter, or mulch piled against siding. Related: Termite Inspection
Crack-and-crevice treatment
Applying small amounts of product directly into cracks, voids and seams where pests hide, rather than across open surfaces. Related: Cockroach Control

D

Dampwood termite
A termite that lives in wet, decaying wood, most common in the Pacific Northwest and coastal areas. Related: Termite Control
Drywood termite
A termite that lives entirely inside dry wood with no soil contact, leaving six-sided fecal pellets; common in coastal southern states. Related: Termite Control

E

Exclusion
Physically sealing gaps, vents and openings so pests and wildlife cannot get into a building. Related: Rodent Control

F

FIFRA
The Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act, the federal law under which the EPA registers pesticides and sets label rules. Related: General Pest Control
Fire ant
An invasive, stinging ant that builds dome-shaped mounds in sunny lawns across the southern United States. Related: Ant Control
Flea dirt
Black specks of digested blood left by adult fleas in pet fur and bedding; they turn red on a damp paper towel. Related: Flea & Tick Control
Formosan termite
An invasive subterranean termite with very large colonies, established along the Gulf Coast, in the Southeast and Hawaii. Related: Termite Control
Frass
Insect droppings or debris. Drywood termite frass is pellet-shaped; carpenter ant frass looks like coarse sawdust. Related: Termite Inspection
Fumigation
Sealing a structure under tarps and filling it with a gas that penetrates wood to kill drywood termites or other pests. Related: Termite Control

G

Gel bait
A pest bait in gel form, placed in small dabs where cockroaches or ants feed, so they carry it back to the colony. Related: Cockroach Control
German cockroach
The most common indoor cockroach in the U.S., small and fast-breeding, usually found in kitchens and bathrooms. Related: Cockroach Control

H

Hantavirus
A group of viruses spread mainly by deer mice, through contact with urine and droppings; cleaning up safely reduces risk. Related: Rodent Control
Harborage
A place where pests hide, rest and breed, such as wall voids, appliance housings or clutter. Related: Cockroach Control
Heat treatment
Raising room temperatures high enough, and long enough, to kill bed bugs and their eggs throughout furniture and walls. Related: Bed Bug Treatment

I

Insect growth regulator
A product, often called an IGR, that mimics insect hormones to stop eggs and young insects from developing into breeding adults. Related: Flea & Tick Control
Integrated pest management
A science-based approach, often called IPM, that combines inspection, monitoring, prevention and targeted treatment to control pests with the least risk. Related: General Pest Control

L

Larvicide
A product that kills insect larvae, used most often in standing water to stop mosquitoes before they become biting adults. Related: Mosquito Control
Liquid termiticide
An insecticide applied to the soil around and under a structure to create a treated zone that stops subterranean termites. Related: Termite Control

M

Monitoring device
A trap or station, such as a glue board or pheromone trap, used to track pest activity over time. Related: Commercial Pest Control
Mud tubes
Pencil-width tunnels of soil that subterranean termites build to travel between the ground and wood. Related: Termite Inspection

N

Non-repellent insecticide
A product insects cannot detect, so they walk through it and spread it to the colony instead of avoiding it. Related: Ant Control
Norway rat
Also called the brown rat, a large, burrowing rat that usually enters buildings at ground level. Related: Rodent Control
NPMA-33
The Wood Destroying Insect Inspection Report form, developed by the National Pest Management Association and used in many real estate sales. Related: Termite Inspection
Nuisance wildlife
Wild animals, such as raccoons, squirrels and bats, that cause damage or health risks by living in or around buildings. Related: Wildlife Removal

O

One-way exclusion door
A device installed over an animal's entry point that lets it leave a building but not get back in. Related: Wildlife Removal

P

Paper wasp
A wasp that builds open, umbrella-shaped paper combs under eaves, railings and grills. Related: Wasp & Bee Removal
Perimeter treatment
A band of product applied around a building's foundation, doors and eaves to stop crawling insects entering. Related: General Pest Control
Pharaoh ant
A tiny, yellowish indoor ant whose colonies split when sprayed, so it is controlled with baits. Related: Ant Control
Pre-construction treatment
Termite treatment of the soil or framing during building construction, before the slab is poured. Related: Termite Control

R

Re-entry interval
The time people and pets must wait before going back into a treated area, set by the product label. Related: General Pest Control
Residual
How long a pesticide keeps working after it is applied. Related: General Pest Control
Rodenticide
A pesticide used to kill rodents, sold to consumers only in tamper-resistant bait stations. Related: Rodent Control
Roof rat
A climbing rat common in southern and coastal states that travels along trees and utility lines and nests in attics. Related: Rodent Control

S

Subterranean termite
A termite that nests in soil and builds mud tubes to reach wood; the most damaging termites in the U.S. Related: Termite Control
Swarmers
Winged reproductive termites or ants that fly from mature colonies to start new ones, often in spring. Related: Termite Inspection

T

Termite bait system
In-ground stations containing cellulose bait and an insect growth regulator that termites share, eliminating the colony over time. Related: Termite Control
Termite bond
A termite warranty or service contract promising re-treatment, and sometimes repair, if termites return. Related: Termite Control

W

Wood-destroying insect
Any insect that damages wood, including termites, carpenter ants, carpenter bees and powderpost beetles. Related: Termite Inspection

Y

Yellow jacket
A black-and-yellow wasp that nests in the ground or in wall voids and becomes aggressive in late summer. Related: Wasp & Bee Removal
