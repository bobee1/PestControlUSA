Spider Exterminator & Spider Control

Most spiders are harmless and even eat other pests, but webs everywhere, black widows in the garage or brown recluses in closets need a plan that removes them and the insects they feed on.

Quick answer: Spider control removes webs and egg sacs, treats cracks, eaves and voids where spiders hide, and reduces the insects they eat by changing outdoor lighting and sealing gaps. Brown recluse and black widow spiders need more careful inspection and glue-board monitoring because they hide in undisturbed clutter.

What's included in spider control

  • Inspection of eaves, garages, basements, closets and storage areas
  • Identification of species, including medically important spiders
  • Web and egg sac removal from eaves, windows and entryways
  • Crack-and-crevice and void treatment with labeled products
  • Perimeter treatment to reduce insects spiders feed on
  • Glue-board monitors for recluse spiders
  • Exclusion advice: sealing gaps, screens and door sweeps

How spider control works, step by step

  1. Inspect and identify. Find where spiders and webs concentrate and confirm whether medically important species are present.
  2. Remove webs and egg sacs. Clearing webs and sacs reduces numbers right away and shows where new webs appear.
  3. Treat hiding spots. Labeled products go into cracks, eaves, voids and corners rather than open surfaces.
  4. Reduce prey. Perimeter treatment and lighting changes cut the insects that draw spiders to the house.
  5. Monitor. Glue boards along walls in closets, basements and garages track recluse activity.
  6. Seal and declutter. Sealing gaps and storing items in sealed plastic bins removes shelter.

Brown recluse spiders: a special case

Brown recluse spiders live mainly in the south-central United States, including Missouri, Kansas, Oklahoma, Arkansas, Tennessee, Kentucky and parts of neighboring states. They hide in undisturbed places such as boxes, closets, shoes and attic insulation, and a heavy infestation can number in the hundreds. Control takes repeated monitoring with glue boards, reducing clutter, sealing cracks and targeted void treatments over several months. Many spider bites reported outside this range are actually other skin conditions, so see a doctor for any wound that worsens.

Lighting, landscaping and spiders

Outdoor lights attract moths and other night-flying insects, which attract web-building spiders to porches and eaves. Switching to yellow or warm-toned LED bulbs, moving lights away from doors, and trimming shrubs back from siding all cut spider activity without any product. Firewood and stored items against the house create the dark, undisturbed spots widows prefer.

What affects the cost of spider control

  • Species involved, with recluse programs needing more visits
  • Home size and amount of eave and web removal
  • Whether spiders are added to an existing general pest plan
  • Clutter and storage areas that need monitoring
  • Exclusion work such as screen and door sweep repairs

Before any spider control work begins, get a written estimate covering species involved, the method, visits and guarantee.

DIY vs. calling a professional

Vacuuming webs and egg sacs, sealing gaps and changing outdoor bulbs handle most house spider issues. Call a professional for black widows or brown recluses in living areas, spiders in children's rooms, heavy web build-up on high eaves, or spiders that keep returning despite cleaning.

Licensing and legal notes

Spider treatments use the same registered insecticides as general pest control and must follow label directions. Seek medical care for any bite from a black widow or brown recluse; a pest control provider cannot give medical advice.

Spider Control questions

Why do I have so many spiders in my house?

Usually because there are plenty of insects for them to eat, plus gaps that let them in. Reducing prey insects and sealing entry points reduces spiders.

Are house spiders dangerous?

Most house spiders are harmless to people. Black widows and brown recluses are the main medically important spiders in the U.S.

How long does spider treatment last?

Exterior treatments typically last several weeks to a few months, depending on weather. That is why spiders are often included in quarterly general pest plans.

What do brown recluse spiders look like?

They are tan to brown with a darker violin-shaped mark on the back and six eyes arranged in pairs, about the size of a quarter including legs.

Should I kill spiders outside my house?

Outdoor spiders away from the home help control insects. Treatment focuses on the structure, entry points and areas where people spend time.
