Wildlife Removal: Raccoons, Squirrels & Bats

Thumping in the attic, chewed vents or bats at dusk usually mean wildlife has moved in. Humane wildlife removal gets animals out, seals the entry and cleans up what they leave behind.

Quick answer: Wildlife removal starts with identifying the animal and every entry point, then uses one-way exclusion doors or live trapping where legal, seals the openings with heavy-duty materials and cleans contaminated insulation. State wildlife agencies regulate trapping and relocation, and bats are protected in many states, so timing and permits matter.

What's included in wildlife removal

  • Inspection of the roof line, vents, chimneys, soffits, crawl spaces and decks
  • Animal identification from tracks, droppings, sounds and damage
  • One-way exclusion doors so animals leave on their own
  • Live trapping where state law allows, with legal handling
  • Baby animal checks and reunions with the mother when possible
  • Sealing entry points with steel mesh, flashing and vent guards
  • Chimney caps and roof vent covers
  • Attic cleanup, sanitizing and insulation replacement referrals

How wildlife removal works, step by step

  1. Inspect. Check the whole structure from roof to foundation and identify the species and every entry point.
  2. Plan legally. Confirm state rules on trapping, relocation and protected species such as bats.
  3. Remove humanely. Install one-way doors so animals exit, or trap where permitted, checking for young first.
  4. Seal entries. Repair and reinforce openings with materials animals cannot chew or pull through.
  5. Clean up. Remove droppings, nesting material and damaged insulation, and sanitize the area.
  6. Prevent. Trim branches back from the roof, cap chimneys and secure trash and pet food.

Bats: exclusion timing and protection

Bats eat large numbers of night-flying insects, and several species are protected under state and federal law. Exclusion is typically done with one-way devices outside the summer maternity season, when flightless pups could be trapped inside. Bats can carry rabies; if a bat is found in a room where someone was sleeping, or with a child or pet, contact your doctor or local health department about rabies exposure before releasing the bat.

Why wildlife control is licensed separately

Most states regulate nuisance wildlife control through the state wildlife or natural resources agency, separate from pesticide licensing. Rules cover who can trap, which species need permits, whether animals may be relocated or must be released on site, and how to handle rabies vector species such as raccoons, skunks, foxes and bats. Ask providers for their wildlife permit or nuisance wildlife control operator license in addition to any pest control license.

Attic contamination and cleanup

Raccoon latrines can contain roundworm eggs, and bat guano can harbor fungal spores, so heavy contamination should be cleaned by trained crews with respirators, not swept up. Cleanup often includes removing soiled insulation, sanitizing framing, and re-insulating. Rodent and wildlife droppings also attract flies and beetles if left in place.

What affects the cost of wildlife removal

  • Species and number of animals
  • Number and height of entry points; roof work costs more
  • Exclusion doors versus trapping and trip charges for trap checks
  • Repair materials and labor
  • Attic cleanup and insulation replacement
  • Seasonal timing, such as bat exclusion windows

Before any wildlife removal work begins, get a written estimate covering species and number of animals, the method, visits and guarantee.

DIY vs. calling a professional

Removing food attractants, securing trash cans and trimming branches away from the roof help prevent wildlife problems. Do not trap or handle wildlife yourself without checking state law, never seal an opening without confirming animals and young are out, and never handle bats or any animal acting sick. Call a licensed wildlife specialist for animals inside the home.

Licensing and legal notes

Wildlife trapping, relocation and euthanasia rules vary widely by state, and some species, including many bats and migratory birds, are protected. Relocating animals is illegal in some states because it can spread disease. Contact your state wildlife agency for rules where you live.

Local note: Armadillos are a wildlife issue across Texas and the Gulf South, while gray squirrels and raccoons cause attic problems nearly everywhere east of the Rockies. Bat exclusion seasons differ by state. See wildlife information by state.

Wildlife Removal questions

How do I know what animal is in my attic?

Timing helps: squirrels are active at dawn and dusk, raccoons at night with heavy thumping, and bats leave at dusk. Droppings and entry damage confirm the species.

Is it legal to relocate wildlife?

It depends on the state and species. Some states allow relocation with permits, others require release on site or prohibit moving rabies vector species.

Will my homeowners insurance cover wildlife damage?

Some policies cover sudden damage from wildlife such as raccoons, while others exclude rodents and insects. Check your policy and document damage with photos.

How long does wildlife exclusion take?

Most animals leave through one-way doors within a few days. Sealing and repairs follow once the space is confirmed empty.

What should I do if a bat is in my house?

Close interior doors, open a window and give it time to leave. If anyone may have been bitten or a bat was found near a sleeping person, contact your health department before releasing it.
