Mosquito Control Service & Yard Treatment

Mosquitoes breed in water as small as a bottle cap and can go from egg to biting adult in about a week in warm weather. A mosquito control service combines source reduction with seasonal yard treatments.

Quick answer: A mosquito yard treatment applies a labeled residual product to the shaded foliage where adult mosquitoes rest, usually every three to four weeks during mosquito season, and treats or removes standing water where larvae develop. The EPA and CDC recommend an integrated approach that starts with removing breeding sites.

What's included in mosquito control

  • Yard inspection for standing water, containers, gutters and low spots
  • Dumping or treating breeding water; Bti larvicide where water can't be removed
  • Barrier treatment of shrubs, under decks and shaded resting areas
  • Egg-laying trap stations where offered
  • Seasonal programs with visits every few weeks
  • One-time event treatments before parties or weddings
  • Pollinator-aware application that avoids flowering plants
  • Advice on screens, repellents and yard maintenance

How mosquito control works, step by step

  1. Inspect. Walk the property to find breeding water and adult resting areas, including neighbors' runoff that affects your yard.
  2. Reduce sources. Dump, drain or cover water-holding items; recommend fixes for drainage and gutters.
  3. Larvicide. Treat water that can't be removed with larvicides such as Bti, which the EPA has concluded does not pose a risk to humans.
  4. Barrier treatment. Apply a labeled adulticide or repellent product to the undersides of leaves and shaded resting spots.
  5. Repeat in season. Visits every three to four weeks keep the barrier in place from spring through fall.
  6. Adjust. Increase visits after heavy rain or for outdoor events.

Mosquito-borne disease and your yard

Mosquitoes in the United States can spread West Nile virus, which the CDC tracks nationwide each season, as well as Eastern equine encephalitis and, in parts of the South and U.S. territories, dengue. Culex mosquitoes that carry West Nile tend to bite at dusk and night, while Aedes species such as the Asian tiger mosquito bite during the day and breed in small containers around homes. Local mosquito control districts and health departments run area-wide surveillance and spraying; private yard treatment adds protection where you actually spend time.

Protecting bees and pollinators

Many mosquito products are toxic to bees. Responsible providers avoid spraying open flowers, treat when bees are not foraging, and follow label wind and drift restrictions. Ask how a provider protects pollinators and whether they will skip flowering beds, vegetable gardens and ponds with fish.

What affects the cost of mosquito control

  • Yard size and how much vegetation needs treatment
  • Program length: single event or full season
  • Visit frequency, usually every three to four weeks
  • Product type: synthetic or botanical
  • Standing water problems that need larviciding or drainage work

Before any mosquito control work begins, get a written estimate covering yard size and how much vegetation needs treatment, the method, visits and guarantee.

DIY vs. calling a professional

Dumping standing water weekly, cleaning gutters and using EPA-registered repellents go a long way. Call a professional for yards next to woods, wetlands or retention ponds, when you are planning outdoor events, or when local health alerts report West Nile activity and you want added protection.

Licensing and legal notes

Area-wide mosquito spraying is done by local mosquito control districts or health departments; private providers treat individual properties. Some states require notification to neighbors or registries before certain outdoor pesticide applications, and labels restrict spraying near water and flowering plants.

Local note: Mosquito season runs nearly year-round in Florida, south Texas and Hawaii and from roughly late spring through early fall in most northern states. Gulf Coast, Southeast and Great Lakes communities with lots of standing water see the heaviest pressure. See mosquito season by state. Mosquito Control questions

How long does a mosquito yard treatment last?

Most barrier treatments are designed to last about three to four weeks. Heavy rain, irrigation and new growth can shorten that.

Is mosquito spraying safe for kids and pets?

When products are applied according to the label, people and pets can return once sprays have dried, usually within about 30 minutes to an hour. Ask your provider for specific re-entry guidance.

Will mosquito treatment kill bees?

It can if sprayed on flowers. Good providers avoid flowering plants and spray resting areas under leaves and in shade instead.

When should I start mosquito treatments?

Start when daytime temperatures consistently reach the 50s and 60s°F in spring, before populations build, and continue until the first hard frost.

Does a mosquito yard treatment get rid of all mosquitoes?

No treatment eliminates every mosquito, since they fly in from surrounding areas. Programs typically reduce biting significantly in the treated yard.
