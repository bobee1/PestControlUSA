Wasp Nest Removal & Bee Removal

Yellow jackets in the lawn, paper wasps under the eaves or a hornet nest in a tree can turn a yard off-limits. Wasp nest removal is safest when a protected professional handles it at the right time of day.

Quick answer: Wasp and hornet nests are usually treated with a labeled dust or foam directly into the nest entrance, ideally at dusk when the colony is inside, then removed once activity stops. Honey bee colonies are different: they are valuable pollinators and are often relocated by a beekeeper rather than killed.

What's included in wasp & bee removal

  • Identification of the insect: yellow jacket, paper wasp, hornet, honey bee, bumble bee or carpenter bee
  • Locating hidden nests in the ground, walls, attics and shrubs
  • Protective-suit treatment of the nest with labeled dusts, foams or aerosols
  • Removal of the nest once the colony is dead, where reachable
  • Wall-void treatment and comb removal advice for nests inside structures
  • Carpenter bee hole treatment and sealing
  • Referral to a beekeeper for honey bee swarms and colonies
  • Advice on preventing nest building next season

How wasp & bee removal works, step by step

  1. Identify. Confirm the species and nest location from a safe distance; the method differs for wasps and bees.
  2. Plan for safety. Keep people and pets away, and plan treatment for dusk or early morning when foragers are inside.
  3. Treat the nest. Labeled insecticidal dust or foam goes directly into the nest entrance or opening.
  4. Confirm and remove. Once activity stops, accessible nests are removed to prevent secondary pests and re-use.
  5. Handle structures. Nests inside walls may need treatment through the entry, then the opening sealed after the colony is dead.
  6. Prevent. Seal gaps, paint or stain bare wood, and remove early-season nests while small.

Why honey bees are handled differently

Honey bees pollinate crops and gardens, and many states and local beekeeping groups maintain swarm-removal lists so colonies can be relocated instead of destroyed. A spring swarm hanging on a branch will often move on within a day or two. Established colonies inside walls are a bigger job, because comb and honey left behind melt, attract other insects and can stain drywall; a cut-out by a beekeeper or contractor is usually needed. Africanized honey bees, found in parts of the Southwest, Texas, Florida and southern California, are more defensive and should only be handled by professionals.

Allergic reactions and sting safety

Most stings cause local pain and swelling, but people with insect venom allergies can have severe whole-body reactions. Call 911 for trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, dizziness or a rapid pulse after a sting. Anyone with a known allergy should stay away from nest treatment entirely and keep prescribed epinephrine on hand.

Why not to seal or flood a nest

Plugging a yellow jacket hole in a wall can force wasps to chew through drywall into the home. Pouring gasoline or water into ground nests is dangerous, pollutes soil and often fails. Treat the entrance with a labeled product, wait for activity to stop, then seal.

What affects the cost of wasp & bee removal

  • Species and colony size
  • Nest location: eave, ground, tree, attic or wall void
  • Height and access, including ladders or lifts
  • Number of nests on the property
  • Comb removal and wall repair for nests inside structures
  • Honey bee relocation, often priced separately by beekeepers

Before any wasp & bee removal work begins, get a written estimate covering species and colony size, the method, visits and guarantee.

DIY vs. calling a professional

A small paper wasp nest the size of a golf ball, reachable from the ground, can be knocked down at dusk with a labeled wasp spray if no one in the home is allergic. Call a professional for yellow jacket ground or wall nests, hornet nests, any nest above ladder height, nests inside structures, honey bee colonies, and any situation involving an allergic person.

Licensing and legal notes

Some states and municipalities protect honey bees or regulate who may remove colonies, and pesticide labels prohibit applying many products to blooming plants where bees forage. Always follow the label and check with your state apiary program or local beekeeping association for swarm removal help.

Wasp & Bee Removal questions

When is the best time to remove a wasp nest?

Dusk or early morning, when most foragers are inside and less active. Early spring nests are smallest and easiest to remove.

Will a wasp nest come back in the same place?

Wasps rarely reuse an old nest, but the same protected spot can attract a new queen. Removing old nests and sealing gaps helps.

Do you kill honey bees?

Honey bee colonies are usually referred to a beekeeper for relocation. Treatment is a last resort, typically for Africanized or inaccessible colonies that pose a safety risk.

How do I know if it's a yellow jacket or a honey bee?

Yellow jackets are smooth, shiny, bright yellow and black with a narrow waist. Honey bees are fuzzy, golden-brown and carry pollen.

Are carpenter bees dangerous?

Males cannot sting and females rarely do. The concern is wood damage over several seasons, which treatment and sealing the holes can stop.
